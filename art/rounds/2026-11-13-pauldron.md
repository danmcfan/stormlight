## Head-to-head: Trellis against the scripted pauldron
Asked by the user ("Would we be better off using Trellis and then just smoothing out the
resulting model?"). No new generation: the existing `plate-pauldron/trellis-v1` mesh.
Built three pauldrons into the same Plate (`PAULDRON_VARIANT=scripted|trellis|hybrid
.venv-blender/bin/python scripts/blender/build_shardplate.py`, `pauldron_variants.py`):
the Trellis mesh with its arm stub cut off, smoothed and decimated, its trim read from its
texture (`trellis_part.Part`); and a hybrid, the same surface carrying the scripted trim. Both
were fitted per axis to the scripted pauldron's bounding box, so size and placement were equal.
Measured (`compare_reference.py --fast`, IoU / lines F / trim F):

| | front | side | back | tris (each) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| scripted | 0.923 / 0.830 / 0.767 | 0.914 / 0.688 / 0.469 | 0.916 / 0.666 / 0.639 | 2,792 |
| Trellis | 0.917 / 0.795 / 0.710 | 0.914 / 0.704 / 0.467 | 0.909 / 0.618 / 0.552 | 3,810 |
| hybrid | 0.918 / 0.827 / 0.764 | 0.913 / 0.646 / 0.422 | 0.910 / 0.645 / 0.605 | 2,824 |

Blind judge (3 runs, variants shuffled to A/B/C): scripted > Trellis > hybrid, 3/3. All three
named the Trellis pieces' torn lower edge (the cut through the lames), lumpy surface,
dashed trim and a flat faceted boss where the drawing has a ringed disc.
Render: `art/renders/shardplate/pauldron-trellis-head-to-head.png`.
Decision: keep the scripted pauldron. Trellis's one real win is that its lames step in 3D,
which is the scripted piece's top judged defect; take that as the target, not the mesh.
Judge defects on the scripted pauldron (3/3): the lames read as one flared skirt with
painted lines, not 3-4 stepped plates; the dome's trim is thin and wanders instead of one
heavy band along its rim; the fin is a flat knife-edged slab apart from the dome, where the
drawing's crest is rounded, trimmed and leans in. (The surface grain all three named is the
4-sample `--fast` render, not the model.)
Next: those three, in that order.
